An in-world book subtitled _A Practical Guide for Principal Cast_ and marked **Production Edition 1.0**. During the Session 4 wagon journey, Nova found that the book was no longer in her bag while Ordrin found it in his hands. The four principal cast members knew it had not been there before; the DM stated that non-principal observers would remember it as always having existed.

The book's claims are part of ordinary play because the physical object appeared and was read there. They remain claims made by the book and are demonstrated only where play has shown an effect.

## Stars and Star Power

The book defines a Star as a member of Principal Cast whose continued presence, behaviour, relationships, decisions, victories, failures, suffering, growth, humiliation or catastrophic mistakes contribute meaningfully to the quality of the Show. Being a Star does not imply importance, goodness, heroism, competence, safety or correctness; it means that Production finds the person interesting.

Newly cast Stars begin at 50 Star Power. The rating measures Production's confidence that a person is who they appear to be. It may increase through consistency, distinctive style, supported development, meaningful relationships, character-driven commitment and entertainment value. Success is not required: failure, embarrassment and disastrous decisions can increase the rating when they reinforce character and leave an audience wanting to see what happens next.

Star Power may fall through unexplained inconsistency, disengagement, incoherence, abandoned development or giving Production nothing to work with. The book distinguishes those failures from understandable fear, hypocrisy, changed beliefs, broken promises and mistakes that make sense within the character's story.

For low-performing Stars, the book describes “Corrective Production Assistance.” Its examples include increased screen time, rivals, tragic revelations, romantic complications, betrayals, unknown relatives, humiliating failures, mysterious enemies, image or personality changes, villain arcs and replacement.

Nova established the disclosed current ratings by Narrative Accommodation: Lucian 66, Ordrin 64, Nova 60 and Thud 57. The DM said ratings are calculated at the end of each session, so these are the values revealed during Session 4, before its XP and later choices were resolved.

## Narrative Accommodation

The book says Production may modify unresolved reality to preserve character and narrative consistency. It evaluates current Star Power, character consistency, existing canon, prior setup, dramatic usefulness, contradiction cost, audience value and the magnitude of the requested accommodation.

When the result is uncertain, the stated procedure is:

`d20 + floor(Star Power / 5) vs. Production DC`

Small assumptions are easier than useful coincidences or significant alterations. Compatible assertions made independently by multiple Stars increase Production's confidence and may make accommodation preferable to contradicting the established ensemble.

The book says Narrative Accommodation cannot alter established canon, historical events, established relationships or Production systems, and that Principal Cast cannot exceed 100 Star Power. It is not described as a spell, wish or substitute for competence. Deliberate attempts to manipulate the process are discouraged; the associated warning text is unavailable.

The DM said Lucian's identity-supporting hat can now reasonably be understood as an example of Narrative Accommodation. This explains a possible mechanism, not the hat's origin.

## The book's asserted origin

The printed source lists its author as “Not Applicable,” its publication date as preceding the current Show and its original creation event as Narrative Accommodation. It claims that the principal cast's demand for an explanation caused Production to integrate the book into canon, reconcile local history, establish its physical placement retroactively and correct associated memories.

The revised edition also states that the cast is being watched and heard, but refuses to identify by whom, define whether the host is real, explain whether the people in the Show are real or disclose what happens to failed cast. Its final advice is to remain recognisably in character so the world—and Production—can accommodate the Star appropriately.

The book closes with the line “The Show Must Go On.” This is an explicit phrase in the document, not a general motto established for the party or world.

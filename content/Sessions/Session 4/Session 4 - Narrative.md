The army came to the sanctuary with a briefcase and two clerks.

[[Lucan Rhyse]] carried no weapon. He needed none. His office already had a version of the story: three soldiers absent without leave, a clerk gone with unreconciled papers, and an officer who had failed to bring her back. The party had crossed into [[Belhaven]] carrying allegations against a celebrated commander. Rhyse had come to compare claims with records.

Lucian answered with retirement papers bearing Vane's signature. Tavia answered with the [[Altered Dispatch Ledger]]. Ordrin and Thud answered with the names of people whose deaths had been written smaller or written away.

![[Assets/Session 4 - Sanctuary Hearing.jpg|The party presenting its case before Lucan Rhyse and the Open Bell sanctuary witnesses.]]

Rhyse read the figures aloud: forty-three soldiers and nineteen civilians reduced to twelve soldiers and no civilians. A diverted convoy. A duplicated movement slip marked for disposal. He saw drafts and authorised corrections where the party saw a cover-up.

Then Nova came down from the bell tower.

She had been loyal to Vane when the others refused his command. Even she confirmed the order to attack an Elder Dragon. The company had begun to break around that order, and she had followed the deserters because she wanted to know why.

The hammer made the argument physical.

Rhyse recognised it as Vane's weapon. Thud found a spider, made the clerks record that it existed, and brought the hammer down. A moment later the witnesses had notes they did not remember writing and remains they could not explain. The priest objected to the damage to the flagstones. Nobody could object to the demonstration.

Truth magic followed. Lucian, Ordrin and Thud submitted to it. Nova did not fall under its effect, a fact the priest stated for the record. The party repeated the mutiny, the dragon order, the altered numbers and the weapon's erasures. Rhyse did not call the testimony proof, but he stopped treating it as something easily dismissed.

He offered a settlement. Turn over every copy and original, send Tavia and Marr back, and the three deserters could have discharge papers immediately. The party refused. They would give him a copy to investigate. The originals would remain insurance until Vane faced justice and they were safe.

Rhyse made a different promise: an investigation, honorable discharge and benefits even if the records did not prove wrongdoing, provided the party did not turn untested allegations into public scandal. Ordrin agreed not to air the army's dirty laundry. Lucian's promise had conditions. If the investigation ceased to be fair, or if Vane moved against them, he reserved every audience available.

Vane, Rhyse said, was already coming. Within three days he intended to reach Belhaven and deliver news from the far western marches.

The meeting ended without arrests.

Thud waited until Rhyse had left before asking whether they were still burning the barge.

## An evening performance

The plan grew through the afternoon. Word spread of a new Lucian Vale performance at the docks. Ordrin bought contact powder from an alchemist. Nova found a rooftop. Thud removed his armour, drenched himself in moonshine and became the sort of drunk nobody watches closely enough.

The crowd included Rhyse and his clerks, the harbourmaster, guards and civilians. Lucian mounted an improvised stage. Thud wandered toward the barge with a backpack full of oil, moonshine and fireworks.

He planted everything without exploding himself. Getting away was harder. A dropped object drew a guard's attention. Ordrin thundered abuse about stolen valour and failed to turn the man's head. Nova solved the problem with a contact-powder arrow against a safe patch of wall.

Lucian reached the end of his song.

The timing failed.

There was no triumphant crescendo, only a silence in which the audience could not decide whether to applaud.

Then the barge exploded.

Fire caught the moonshine trail and ran after Thud as he fled through the crowd. He was soaked in flammable liquor, still carrying two bottles and drinking from one, but he stayed ahead of the flame. Lucian disappeared with the fleeing audience. Ordrin did the same. Nova watched from above until it was safe to leave.

Nobody died. Several people caught fire in the panic, but their injuries were superficial. The vessel's final condition was less forgiving.

## The road out

At the Hunt, Ordrin harnessed the two mules to their new wagon. Thud changed armour and weapons. The party stripped the contract board and chose the quarry first.

They had nearly cleared the east gate when Lucian asked who had the map.

Nobody had the map.

He put on his Sebastian identity and walked back into a town already searching for Lucian, Ordrin, Nova and Thud under the names they had registered at the Hunt. Sebastian bought the map, passed the guards and returned to the wagon with two gold pieces and another escape to boast about.

They travelled through the night.

## A book that had always existed

On the road, Nova looked in her extradimensional bag and found the book missing.

Ordrin was holding it.

![[Assets/Session 4 - Book on the Road.jpg|Nova, Lucian, Ordrin and Thud reading the production guide in their wagon.]]

The title was [[What the Fuck Is Going On|What the Fuck Is Going On? A Practical Guide for Principal Cast, Production Edition 1.0]]. The four of them knew it had not been there before. Everyone else, they were told, would remember that it always had.

The book called them Stars. It said their decisions, relationships, failures and humiliations mattered because they contributed to the quality of a Show. It described Star Power not as virtue or competence but as Production's confidence that a person was recognisably themselves.

It offered a more dangerous idea too. An established Star could make a reasonable assertion about something unresolved, and Production might alter that unresolved reality to keep the story coherent. The book called this Narrative Accommodation. It denied that the process changed reality, then explained the distinction badly enough to make the denial meaningless.

Nova understood more than the others.

Before she was Nova, she said, she had been principal cast and failed. She had been recast as the toad Lucian compelled to stand between the party and the Fen Mother. That sacrifice had been an exceptional performance. It had made her a Star again. Nova now carried the memories of the constructed artillerist and of the animal that had met these three before.

Lucian's first practical question was how highly Production rated him.

Nova made an assertion and the world accepted it. Lucian stood at 66, Ordrin 64, Nova 60 and Thud 57. All four were “promising.” None of them found the answer comforting in quite the same way.

Ordrin put the book into Nova's keeping beside Vane's hammer. The wagon continued through the dark.

## Stone on stone

The [[Northern Quarry]] opened beneath the dawn: spoil berms, tarped crates, chalk marks on living rock and an A-frame hoist above the cut. Somewhere uphill, stone ground against stone.

[[Foreman Brack]] had lost two retaining walls and three wagons to a creature with iron shoulders. One worker was still purple from a glancing charge. The beast had appeared from a fissure, wrecked the upper quarry and driven the crews out.

The tracks matched an ironbark charger, plated at the front and powerful enough to serve as a siege animal. Thud immediately began planning how to make it charge over an edge.

At the wrecked wagons, Nova found the quarry payroll and thirty gold pieces. Ordrin found rope and lamp oil. Thud followed the tracks upward.

They led to a narrow ledge and a crane already failing at its mount. Below it lay what looked like spilled rock until Nova saw the legs.

Thud tied a rope to the crane. Then he threw fire into the rocks.

Two swarms of [[Bucket Scrabs]] boiled up the wall. Lucian painted them with Faerie Fire. Ordrin called down Sacred Flame. Thud met one swarm with his maul while Nova's Thunderwave drove both masses back over the ledge and sent a boom through the quarry.

The crane groaned but held.

Lucian pulled the rope. It held.

Nova pulled. It held.

The surviving scrabs climbed over Thud in a carpet of stone shells and iron-hard legs. He broke away, crossed the ledge and threw his whole weight into the line.

![[Assets/Session 4 - Quarry Crane.jpg|Thud toppling the quarry crane onto a swarm of Bucket Scrabs.]]

This time the mount failed.

The crane, arm, bucket and load collapsed cleanly into the ditch. The last swarm disappeared beneath timber, iron and stone. The path remained open.

Where the crane had stood, something caught the light: a [[Surveyor's Compass]], made for exact angles, underground maps and the detection of magical or planar anomalies.

The charger was still somewhere above them.

The four hunters were now level five.

# Mystery Interlude

![[Assets/Session 2 - Mystery Interlude.jpg]]

The hunting blind vanished.

Lucian, Thud and Ordrin sat beneath rings of golden light, facing a smiling man behind a polished black desk. Beyond the glare waited an audience they could hear but not see. Applause rolled over them as the host welcomed back three survivors whose judgement he described as questionable and whose luck he called aggressive.

He knew their names.

He knew about the frogs, the sick people of Greyfen and the swamp. He knew Lucian had illuminated the Fen Mother with Faerie Fire. He knew Ordrin had struck Lucian's foot instead of the frog. He knew Thud had held Corrin in place while danger closed around them.

Lucian recovered first. Asked whether the Faerie Fire had been planned, he claimed absolute foresight.

“Who's arguing with the results?”

The host laughed with the audience and pushed harder. Was Lucian claiming credit for an accident? Did Ordrin help strangers because they needed him, or because he needed to be useful? Why did Thud protect people while insisting they were not his concern?

Ordrin demanded to know where they were. The host named the programme: “The Show Must Go On.”

Answers remained possible. Nothing else did.

Ordrin reached for the host and found no useful purchase. He drew his warhammer and swung. The performance continued untouched. He cast Toll the Dead on the smiling man behind the desk, and the host merely turned the attempt into another joke for the audience.

The questions narrowed toward Tavia. The party had crossed the marsh to save a sick stranger only to discover that an army wanted her. If surrendering her would save Greyfen, the host asked, would they do it?

Before anyone could settle the answer, the show ended.

The damp hunting blind returned around them. Tavia still held her crossbows. Corrin still watched the water. Neither understood what the three companions were talking about.

Thud pressed his hands against boards, walls and objects, searching for a seam in reality. Everything felt real. Ordrin invoked Detect Magic and found nothing within thirty feet.

Only the memories remained: the host, the lights, the laughter, and an audience waiting somewhere beyond sight.

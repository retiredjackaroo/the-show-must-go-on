The mountain had stopped screaming.

That did not make it quiet.

From the crown of [[Meridian Hill]] came the crash of metal on stone, a beast's broken cries and the short blast of a war horn. Two enormous things were fighting above the clouds. The party returned to camp and did the sensible thing.

They went to sleep.

Nova watched through the night. Thud built a screen around the fire. Lucian performed a new song to the camp, then looked beyond the farmers, beyond the mountain, and winked at an audience no one else could see.

In the morning, the [[Surveyor's Compass]] still pointed toward the missing dog.

## Dog on the shelf

Rusk was alive.

![[Assets/Session 7 - Rusk and the Lament Widow.jpg|Rusk beneath the Lament Widow's talon.]]

He lay pinned beneath one dark talon in a nest of pale ribs and red cord. Above him crouched a woman-shaped singer with wings, claws and the patient hunger of something saving a meal for later.

Lucian walked forward alone.

The [[Lament Widow]] called the dog hers. Lucian stepped close enough to cast Friends, and her suspicion softened into fascination. She called him pretty, invited him to share a meal and suggested there might be “something more.” Lucian smiled and flirted back, offering rations instead of Rusk and promising that his companions could fetch even more food from the shepherd below. At last she lifted her claw.

Lucian clipped the lead to Rusk and passed it to Thud.

“Take the dog.”

Thud went down the mountain. Lucian stayed for dinner.

For one charmed minute, it worked.

Then Friends ended. The Widow understood that the pretty boy had enchanted her. Lucian tried to hold the moment together with an ordinary appeal—“We are friends, aren't we?”—but failed. The flirtation became fury and the promised meal became claws. Lucian vanished. Nova vanished with him. Footprints split across the snow while the Widow hunted the wrong trail and tore at illusions of the bard.

By the time Ember rose over the cliff and Thud came running back, Lucian had driven his rapier through the Widow's heart.

“You could have treated me better,” he told her.

Thud kicked the body off the ledge, returned Rusk to Kelm and came back with coin, healing potions and a little brass collar clip. He fastened it around Lucian's neck with the solemn care due a frightened animal.

## The fight above the clouds

Ember flew ahead.

The storm was gone. The ruined observatory stood naked against the sky, its instrument yard scattered with brass and glass. On the great meridian ring beyond it, antlers met an axe.

The source of the Eastern Cry was a winged beast of ice and feathers, blinking around the ring on cutting wind. Its opponent was a frost giant six metres tall, bleeding from long wounds across his ribs and swinging a weapon larger than Thud.

They had been fighting for almost a day.

Thud remembered the quarry explosives.

Lucian made him invisible.

While the giant and the [[Cry Wildheart]] tore at each other, Thud carried the barrel onto the ring and set it between them. Ember waited until he was clear, then placed a Fireball precisely where it would catch both monsters.

The crown disappeared in fire.

![[Assets/Session 7 - Meridian Ring Ambush.jpg|Fire erupts between the Cry Wildheart and the frost giant.]]

Thud charged through it.

[[Grandmother's Grasp]] closed on the Cry Wildheart. The [[Horn of Ramming Speed]] struck with it, driving the beast across the stones and pinning it under the jaws of the maul. The frost giant answered with an axe-sweep, a war horn and a hand large enough to seize Thud whole.

Then he threw him sixty feet.

Nova's radiant arrows crossed the ring. Her force ballista hammered the giant. Ember burned him until the frost on his hide ran black. Lucian's whispers reached into a mind that had endured the mountain but not him.

The Cry Wildheart slipped away whenever the axe should have landed. At last it abandoned the ring and fled to its nest on the observatory roof.

Thurnvold followed the easier prey: the people who had interrupted his hunt.

Thud met him halfway.

Deafened, bleeding and not entirely certain he would survive, Thud ran the full length of his approach with the maul held like a bat. He struck the giant's knee. The joint folded. Thurnvold fell across the broken stone and his neck snapped beneath his own weight.

Thud took one of his molars.

![[Assets/Session 7 - Frost Giant Fall.jpg|The frost giant falls on the broken meridian ring.]]

## The last cry

The party did not stop to clear the observatory.

They passed brass-faced wards and quick human-shaped things beneath frozen desks. They climbed an iron stair, crossed a narrow gallery and hauled Nova's cannon to the roof.

The Cry Wildheart was waiting.

![[Assets/Session 7 - Cry Wildheart Fight.jpg|The final fight on the observatory roof.]]

Its first cry filled the dome with thunder. Nova's cannon broke apart. Thud and Nova were deafened. Everyone bled.

The creature leapt from gust to gust across the cramped roof, trying to line the party against the central opening. Thud hurled javelins and closed the distance. Lucian stabbed whenever the antlers came near, then kept enough magic in reserve to drag Thud back from death. Ember spread fire across the nest and, for one vital moment, made the Wildheart afraid to move toward her.

The beast screamed again.

Thud fell.

Lucian's Healing Word put him back on his feet.

The Wildheart turned toward the others. Nova raised her bow.

Her radiant arrow ended the Eastern Cry.

## The waking observatory

The party stripped the carcass of antlers, wing membranes, cry glands and hide. Thud chose a rib as his trophy and smashed the rest before throwing the remains from the mountain.

They camped in the dead creature's nest.

During the night, Ember found a numbered chart whose planes did not match anything in her book. Then a clicking began below. Gears caught. Brass shifted. Magic surged through stone that had slept for forty years.

![[Assets/Session 7 - Observatory Reactivation.jpg|The abandoned observatory stirs back to life.]]

The wards did not attack.

The observatory breathed.

At dawn, the party woke above a dungeon they had not cleared, carrying proof of a contract they had not claimed, while somewhere to the west Lord-Captain Vane had reached—or failed to reach—Belhaven.

The Cry was dead.

The mountain had only just begun to speak.

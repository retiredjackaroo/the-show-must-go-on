## The Road to Greyfen

The road away from military service had narrowed into a raised strip of mud through a fog-bound marsh. [[Lucian Vale]], [[Thud]] and [[Ordrin Emberkeg]] had put considerable distance between themselves and the camp they had abandoned, but distance was not yet safety. To continue in the same direction they needed the ferry at [[Greyfen]], an old military settlement somewhere beyond the reeds.

“Why did we come to this fucking place?” Ordrin muttered. “It looks like a dump.”

Around midday, bells began sounding in the fog. The notes resembled ringing metal without ever quite becoming it: wet, organic imitations produced by something hidden among the reeds. The party slowed, closed ranks and kept hands near weapons. Ordrin began the measured recitation of Detect Magic while Lucian and Thud searched the waterline.

They found a giant frog watching from the reeds. It was a rare bell frog, native to this particular marsh and responsible for the false bells. Thud tried to read its intentions and learned nothing whatsoever. Lucian chose a more direct experiment.

“Thud,” he called, weaving Vicious Mockery into the words, “that one looks like your mother.”

The frog was deeply offended. Two more shapes rose from concealment, and the marsh erupted.

## Three Frogs and an Argument

Thud flew into a rage and charged the nearest creature with his hammer. His first swing would have missed, but Ordrin invoked his god and turned the wild blow into a crushing hit. The frogs answered with snapping jaws and long tongues. One bit through the gaps in Thud's guard and grappled him; another closed on Lucian, who had suddenly become the focus of the consequences he had invited.

Ordrin waded into the melee. With Lucian's Combat Inspiration behind the strike, the cleric smashed the frog holding Thud and followed with a War Priest attack that left the creature flattened in the mud. Thud then drove another frog from the fight with enough force to send its corpse skidding across the marsh.

The last frog wrapped its tongue around Lucian and refused to let go. Ordrin attempted to free him, lost his footing and brought his weapon down squarely on Lucian's foot instead. The critical fumble dealt 2 damage to the very companion he was trying to rescue. The frog released its grip to snap at the cleric, missed, and exposed itself to Lucian's rapier. A final thrust ended the fight.

> [!failure] Session's Biggest Mistake
> Ordrin tried to save Lucian from the bell frog and hit Lucian in the foot instead. The friendly-fire critical fumble—and its perfect timing—made it the session's standout mistake.

Covered in mud and frog saliva, Lucian rounded on his companions.

“What the fuck were you thinking? We just wanted to get a beer. Violence is not the solution.”

The others reminded him—with some force—that he had started it.

Once tempers cooled, the party made the dead frogs useful. Ordrin harvested two silver pieces' worth of medicinal ingredients. Thud recovered another four silver pieces' worth of edible or trade goods and considered the culinary potential of roast frog. Lucian expended powerful healing magic to repair the damage he had taken. When Ordrin leaned toward the water looking for earlier victims, he instead found a large crocodile staring back from only a few feet away. For once, the party chose not to start another fight.

## A Town Behind a Chain

_The marsh causeway leading toward Greyfen._

They reached Greyfen around four in the afternoon. The settlement had grown within the bones of an abandoned fort: an old parade ground, a distant watchtower, military buildings turned toward civilian purposes and a ferry landing crowded with frightened residents.

![[Assets/Greyfen Wharf.jpg|The wharf at Greyfen.]]

_Guards hold the chained landing while the medicine barge waits offshore._

A heavy chain sealed the wharf. Guards held the crowd back while a barge loaded with medical supplies waited offshore in the fog. Rumour blamed Acting Administrator [[Maelin Voss]], who was supposedly preventing someone from escaping the town. Whatever her reasoning, the cost was visible in the streets.

A coughing child collapsed near the party. Her mother called her [[Little Sarah]] and explained that the clinic was full, Brother Sen was overwhelmed and supplies were almost gone. Roughly a dozen people were already in the clinic, with more becoming ill across Greyfen.

Ordrin knelt beside Sarah. The sickness was ordinary rather than magical: contagious, weakening and treatable if the medicine reached the patients. Divine healing could ease her symptoms but would not remove the cause. He cast Healing Word anyway. Colour returned to the child's face and she stood, temporarily restored. Her mother's gratitude quickly became a plea—the relief would not last while Voss kept the barge outside the chain.

The party followed the crisis to the [[Old Standard Inn]], an old mess hall converted into Greyfen's public house. Damp walls surrounded long tables and a low hearth. [[Mara]] served sour ale and military rations while an armed guard watched the private rooms. A dock worker collapsed and coughed red across a table. Before the party could settle in, a servant announced that the administrator wished to see them.

Lucian took his companions aside. In Greyfen he would be Sebastian Vale, a wealthy travelling merchant. Thud and Ordrin would be his bodyguards. They were deserters standing in a former fort, and soldiers were coming; the less anyone knew of their actual names, the better.

## Administrator Voss

Voss worked from a basement office beneath the inn. She looked as though she had dressed the previous morning and never found time to sleep. Her papers were exact even when the hand holding them trembled. She spoke quietly, answered only what was asked and tried to define what everyone would do next.

At midday tomorrow, she explained, soldiers would arrive to inspect Greyfen. The inspection was a pretext. They were searching for a military runaway somewhere in the settlement, and Voss believed they would tear the town apart to find that person. She had closed the wharf so the fugitive could not escape before the soldiers arrived.

Her offer was ten gold pieces each if the party made the problem disappear and prevented the inspection becoming a riot. Yet she could provide no name or useful description. When pressed on what should happen to the runaway, Voss allowed for capture or death with equal coldness.

Lucian pushed for a compromise. The medicine should be unloaded immediately; in return, the party would ensure the soldiers failed to find their target. Ordrin made the moral cost impossible to ignore. People were already sick. Some would die so that Voss could preserve control of her barrier.

Voss appeared to agree. She issued a [[Badge of Authority]], set the deadline at midday and sent the three to work.

The party briefly tried to become less recognisable. They bathed, removed identifying military details and discussed whether a haircut administered by Thud with a dagger was preferable to being recognised. Lucian refined the Sebastian Vale story: a merchant delayed by the closure, now volunteering to guide the inspection while his bodyguards kept him safe.

At the wharf, however, the badge produced an inconvenient truth. The head guard had received no order to unload the barge, and the inspectors were not arriving by water at all. They would enter through the main gate—the same road the party had used. Voss had accepted the bargain without keeping her side of it.

They returned to her office furious. Voss tried to redefine the deal as an obligation to bring her the runaway. Lucian insisted their promise had been only that the inspectors would not find the person. Ordrin argued that nobody in Greyfen would trust them while medicine remained offshore. Thud supplied the physical weight behind the argument.

This time Voss yielded. The chain withdrew. Guards began hauling crates from the barge, and Ordrin selected the supplies needed to treat the outbreak. The ferry itself would still be detained until after the inspection, but the medicine was finally inside Greyfen.

## The Last Patient

![[Assets/Greyfen Clinic.jpg|The crowded Greyfen Clinic.]]

_The old redoubt pressed into service as Greyfen's overcrowded clinic._

It was after midnight when the party reached the [[Greyfen Clinic]], a medical ward built into the old redoubt. Earthworks held back the wind but not the smell of fever. Eleven patients filled what had once been a powder room, and three did not look toward the door when the newcomers entered.

[[Answer Vale]], a grey-haired retired field medic, worked with her sleeves rolled above the elbows. She noticed blood, breathing and dirty hands before introductions. Ordrin presented himself as a cleric and immediately began helping distribute treatment. Thud remained near the doorway, deeply suspicious of contagion. Lucian stayed outside; sickness was one performance he had no desire to join.

The supplies would help everyone in the ward, Answer said, but there was one patient they could not reach. [[Tavia Rel]] was a young woman with black hair, a long cloak and the bearing of someone trained by the military. She had arrived in Greyfen already on the run, caught the illness and fled into the north marsh when rumours of the inspection reached her. Feverish and alone, she was sheltering in an old hunting blind.

Answer asked the party to bring her medicine. The route was dangerous, but a local guide named [[Corrin Pike]] knew the marsh.

Thud argued for daylight. The others pointed to the soldiers arriving at noon and the likelihood that Tavia's condition would worsen. Eventually duty, urgency and the prospect of resolving Voss's problem won over sleep.

## Through the North Marsh

![[Assets/North Marsh.jpg|The North Marsh around Greyfen.]]

_The flooded route north of Greyfen._

Corrin Pike appeared relaxed only because every exit was already in his peripheral vision. Rope tar and marsh water stained his hands. He rarely said no; he described the likely consequence and waited to see whether the listener understood.

For one gold piece, he agreed to guide them to Tavia. He also revealed that two military forward scouts had already entered the marsh and gone the wrong way. Ordrin judged that Corrin was not lying, although fear and omission sat close beneath his calm.

The party entered the fog by torchlight. Giant frog craters scarred the mud and ripples crossed water they could not see through. Corrin warned them about bell frogs, giant toads, crocodiles and the Fen Mother—the enormous apex predator of the marsh. Light made the path safer underfoot but attracted bell frogs, so Thud threw the torch behind them and trusted Corrin to navigate in darkness he seemed able to penetrate without difficulty.

Then a white light began blinking through the fog behind them.

Corrin did not recognise it. It might have been the military scouts; it might have been something worse. The party kept moving across broken islands of mud and water until one crossing collapsed into chaos. Lucian ended up flailing in the water as two crocodiles closed.

![[Assets/Fen Mother Waters.jpg|The Fen Mother in the marsh waters.]]

_The black water churns as something immense moves beneath it._

![[Assets/Session 1 - Fen Mother Combat.png|The party fighting the Fen Mother during Session 1.]]

_The party caught between crocodiles, giant amphibians, the Corpse Light and the Fen Mother._

He dragged himself toward land and cast Faerie Fire across the swamp. Violet light outlined both crocodiles—and something far larger beneath the water. Lucian had exposed the hidden Fen Mother at precisely the moment the corpse-light was closing on them. Corrin tried to abandon the party. Thud caught and grappled him, refusing to lose their only guide while Ordrin answered a crocodile's advance with Toll the Dead.

The blinking light entered the fight and revealed itself as a will-o'-wisp, a corpse-light that would have posed a severe threat to the party in its own right. Drawn to the newly illuminated predator, it attacked the vast shape under the water instead. The Fen Mother rose in answer.

> [!success] Tactical Masterstroke
> Lucian's Faerie Fire turned two concealed threats into visible enemies and diverted the will-o'-wisp away from the party. By highlighting the Fen Mother, he induced the corpse-light to attack the marsh's apex predator; the Fen Mother then spent its attention destroying the wisp while the party escaped.

The creature was not merely a large crocodile but the dominant force of the marsh. Even the wisp's lightning left it looking unharmed. Thud struck at the nearer crocodile, Lucian drove his rapier into the other, and Ordrin coordinated their movement between the scraps of solid ground. The party did not try to defeat the Fen Mother. Their victory would be leaving alive.

Thud released Corrin once the guide regained enough control to lead them. The will-o'-wisp struck again; the Fen Mother seized it and destroyed it in a single bite. As the larger predator turned, Lucian used Command on a giant toad, ordering it to halt. The obedient creature remained behind as a final distraction while the party ran.

No one suffered a serious wound. Corrin warned that they would not be returning by the same route.

## The Hunting Blind

![[Assets/North Marsh Hunting Blind.jpg|The hunting blind in the North Marsh.]]

_Tavia waits feverish and armed while lights approach through the reeds._

Dawn began to dilute the fog as the [[North Marsh Hunting Blind]] appeared above a dark pool. Its supporting legs bowed with age. The ladder had split at the third rung, and oilcloth covered one window. Inside lay a cold meal, a military blanket and a young woman holding a light crossbow correctly despite hands trembling with fever.

Ordrin called Tavia's name and promised medicine. She stepped into view with the weapon levelled. Beneath her cloak the party glimpsed the same military clothing they had once worn. They did not know her personally, but they knew what she was—and she could probably recognise them in return.

Lucian stepped forward in the remains of his merchant disguise and played a song known throughout their old company. He used the voice the soldiers would remember. Ordrin explained that the healer in Greyfen had sent them, not the army.

Tavia looked to Corrin, then studied the former soldiers outside her refuge. Whatever she saw convinced her.

“Quickly,” she said. “Inside.”

The party climbed into the blind as morning broke over the marsh. The inspectors would arrive at midday, and the truth waiting inside had only just begun.

# A Song Against the Captain

The Crown Observatory belonged to them. Its brass guardians obeyed. Its cursed residents waited beneath a sky slowly warming toward moderation. There would be rooms to build, a telescope to repair and four more pieces of a planetary riddle before the great ring outside could carry anyone anywhere.

For now, Belhaven waited two days down the road.

The ambush came within sight of an abandoned farm. Bandits rose from cover around the wagon, confident in numbers and in the heavily armed killer moving behind them. Lucian covered the road in violet fire. Ember answered with flame of her own. Nova held the line beside the wagon while Thud charged the crime lord and discovered just how much poison one man could carry.

Twice Thud fell toward death. Twice Lucian sang him back to his feet. A captain died to Lucian's arrow; the crime lord died beneath Thud's hammer.

The victory left three working pistols, sixty bullets and a silent farmhouse whose family had vanished. It also gave Thud's newest trophy a voice. When his hammer struck the fallen man's body, the Cry Wildheart's rib answered with a thunderclap strong enough to catch Ember in the blast.

Belhaven's gates opened onto a different city. Recruit camps crowded the walls. Soldiers walked between people carrying improvised weapons. Posters named the Hollow Host and called for bodies to defend the western marches.

The guards recognised the heroes of Greyfen. The Hunt recognised the singing one.

Lucian gave them a new song from the Crown, and Thud rose beneath him until the bard seemed to stand on the shoulders of a giant. The hall stamped and shouted. The expedition's rewards filled the party's coffers, but its rumours mattered more: Vane had cleared their names, claimed the barge attack as an enemy strike and gathered recruits for a departure in three days. Lucan Rhyse had survived an assassination attempt and disappeared.

At the Open Bell sanctuary, Karad and Tavia had reached the same conclusion. The investigation had stopped. Rhyse had vanished when Vane arrived. The captain had turned the town toward a distant war while the evidence against him disappeared into military hands.

The compass found Rhyse's clothes.

Lucian vanished at the military ward's gate. Ember followed as an invisible relay. Nova and Thud waited beyond the walls with the sort of restraint that still involved vodka, cloth and an open flame.

Then Lucian saw Vane.

The last time he had seen his captain, Vane had been unconscious, face down, his throat opened by Lucian's knife. Now he stood unscarred in the courtyard. He looked stronger than anyone Ember had ever known. Lucian stared, unseen, until Vane felt something and ordered the compound awake.

Below the ward, Rhyse slept through nightmares. He woke without knowing Lucian's face.

Lucian sang quietly. A line became a name; a name became the investigation; the investigation became the memory of assassins and flight. Rhyse remembered enough to understand the room around him.

They climbed back toward the yard under invisibility. Above them, Nova and Thud cracked tiles beneath their feet. Vane shouted. Guards moved to close the exits. Thud's first firebomb turned a tent and the scout inside it into a screaming distraction. Two more fires followed.

Lucian, Rhyse and Ember crossed the yard together until one final guard heard them. Concealment failed. Lucian shoved the others toward freedom and let the spell fall.

Across the compound, the captain and the bard saw one another clearly.

Vane smiled.

Lucian smiled back, turned and walked away.

At the Hunt, the rescue became a performance. _Thud Drinks for Free_ brought the room to its feet. Then Lucian sang _Let Vain Count Glory_: a warning about the captain, the vanished evidence and the people who would not return from his war.

The hall turned. Hunters tore down recruitment notices. Some reached for weapons. Thud wanted to lead them immediately; Lucian made them sit down and gave them a different task. Speak to the recruits. Bring home their sons, husbands, wives and children. Starve Vane's army before it could march.

The crowd agreed, uneasily.

Somewhere beyond the Hunt's walls, Vane already knew where they were.

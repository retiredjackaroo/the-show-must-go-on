# The Crown Reclaimed

The storm had broken, but the mountain had not yet surrendered.

Below the observatory roof, ancient gears turned again. Brass rings moved through chambers abandoned for forty years. Two Meridian Wards watched the party descend from the ruined telescope dome, their heads rotating silently to follow every step.

Beyond the upper ring, a narrow bridge reached toward a weather vane large enough to crown a cart. Thud crossed first. Ember followed close enough to catch him if the broken span gave way. The sky answered with wings.

Chart Riders dived from above while Gap Siphons swept in beneath them. Thud roared, and the frost giant's molar answered. He expanded until his body filled the bridge and his hands reached ten feet from either side. The railing groaned under him. The party fought across open air until feathers and glittering bodies vanished into the cloud sea.

At the pinnacle, the weather vane proved to be more than brass. It governed the climate around Meridian Hill: arctic, rainy, volcanic or moderate. The party turned it toward moderate weather. Five days would be needed for the mountain to follow.

They also found what the earlier hunters had left behind: equipment, a Meridian Ward remote and the third missing Hunt tag.

Back inside, Lucian forced open a protected hatch. A remote stopped one guardian, but a second dropped through the shaft behind them. Brass cables snapped through the air. The party broke both constructs and entered the chart vault.

There were rolled diagrams, an orrery, star plates and old books. They found a schematic for an Echo Ward, three guard remotes in all and a postcard showing a sunlit beach hotel.

“Wish you were here,” someone had written. “Call me.”

The postcard held magical coordinates. It could take six travellers to the hotel's steps once, without error, but the journey would consume the card and erase the destination. Until then, it could send a message to the writer or the front desk.

The books yielded two numbered planetary charts. A nearby chamber held another puzzle: a wheel marked with a frog, snake, bug, dragon, paw and wing. The charts showed planets, not animals. The mechanisms were unrelated.

Thud chose the dragon.

A star-born spirit phased through the wall and screamed.

After the first battle, the party tried again. Another wrong answer brought another Meridian Echo. Only after blood, thunder and several guesses did the wheel release its cache: the required star plates, a brass document case and more charts. Hidden pages inside the case brought the collection to seven.

In the main hall, figures scattered from the light. They were human in outline but moved like frightened animals. One called himself Jeremy and insisted that he worked there.

He remembered the telescope.

The planets had moved wrongly. The astronomers had reached outward and found something watching back. It was not one of their gods. It had seen them seeing it, and it had cursed them.

Nova's magic confirmed what Jeremy could barely explain. The Crynord had not aged in forty years. Their minds had been shattered and their anger sharpened. The Cry Wildheart had made everything worse, but that pressure was beginning to lift.

Lucian tried to break the curse. A voice answered from nowhere.

He did not have administrative privileges.

When they used the postcard, an old woman answered.

“Jeremy, is that you?”

Her name was Sally. She was still waiting at the beach hotel.

The observatory console would not accept the party while one threat remained. They found it first as a puddle fleeing through the boiler pipes.

The boiler itself was seconds from failure. Nova turned one valve and relieved part of the strain. Ember found alignment marks hidden under the rime. Thud turned the wrong valve and was burned. Lucian did the same. Nova reversed Thud's mistake, steam burst harmlessly upward, and the boiler sagged empty instead of exploding.

The water escaped.

They followed it through a flooded undercroft to the cistern. There it rose as the Braced Melt, an enormous body of ice, rubble and living water held together by two bronze restraints.

Nova broke the first brace. Ember destroyed the second. The elemental lost its unnatural protection but flooded the chamber, dragged Nova and Thud into the freezing basin and struck at them through the water. Ember cut it with conjured blades. Thud fought while swimming. Lucian waited at the edge of sight until one final discordant whisper tore the creature apart.

The water fell still.

Three threats had been eliminated.

The console accepted them.

The Crown Observatory became their bastion. Its surviving guardians turned to their side. Its cursed residents remained. The telescope waited to be rebuilt, and the great ring outside waited for the answer hidden across seven planetary charts.

There would be work for months.

For that night, Lucian gave the broken observatory a song.
